SONAR — Final Scientific Consolidation, RES Model Finalization, and Codex Handoff
1. Mission and Overall Objectives
You are continuing the development of SONAR (System for Optical Normalization and Analysis of RNA Editing), a four-layer quantitative measurement framework developed for the SUSTech-China iGEM 2026 project.
The latest completed scientific analysis is provided as:
SONAR_S7_S10_Layer23_v4_label_corrected_20261013.zip
This archive contains the corrected S7/S10 reconstruction, Layers 0–3 implementation, calibration analyses, molecular measurements, validation results, source code, and scientific reports.
Your task is to complete the final scientific consolidation of SONAR before handing the project to Codex for software engineering and user-interface development.
This is not another reconstruction from scratch.
It is not a request for a new theoretical framework.
It is an execution task intended to produce a scientifically coherent, reproducible, and software-ready measurement system.
The existing four-layer architecture must be preserved:
Layer	Name	Primary function	Output
Layer 0	Context Calibration	Establish a reproducible fluorescence measurement context	Context profile
Layer 1	Flow Quantification	Quantify reporter-normalized fluorescence	RAS
Layer 2	Reporter Calibration	Convert RAS to reference-equivalent reporter activity	RES
Layer 3	Biological Calibration	Evaluate and, where justified, predict molecular RNA editing	PER


Strategic priorities
The final SONAR system should be centered on three established or actively developed contributions:
First: reliable fluorescence quantification.
Layers 0–1 should provide a reproducible method for extracting interpretable reporter responses from multicolor flow-cytometry data.
Second: reference-calibrated measurement.
Layer 2 and RES should become the principal mathematical modeling contribution.
This is the highest priority of the current task.
Third: molecular evaluation.
Layer 3 should retain the complete molecular reconstruction, Sanger analysis, modeling attempts, and actual validation results, while clearly distinguishing relative molecular signals from quantitatively validated RNA-editing percentages.
The objective is not to make every layer appear equally successful.
Instead, develop a complete, scientifically defensible measurement system whose strongest quantitative contributions are reproducible RAS measurement and empirical RES calibration.

4. Phase II — Final Refinement of Layer 1 and RAS
Objective
Preserve the established Reporter Activity Score while strengthening its statistical evaluation and preparing it for direct software use.
The primary estimator remains:
\[
\boxed{
RAS_{\mathcal P}
=
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}g_i}
{\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}r_i}
}
\]
where \(g_i\) and \(r_i\) are the background-corrected linear EGFP and mCherry intensities.
This definition must not be changed merely to improve calibration performance.
4.1 Preserve both analysis populations
Retain:
\[
\mathcal P_R=\mathcal V\cap R_q^+
\]
and:
\[
\mathcal P_{RB}=\mathcal V\cap R_q^+\cap B^+
\]
The first measures the reporter-normalized response of the reporter-expressing population.
The second measures reporter response conditional on detectable editor-linked BFP expression.
Report both when supported.
Do not force samples lacking editor-linked BFP through the co-expression gate.
Do not treat BFP fluorescence as an exact measure of active ORCA abundance.
4.2 Reevaluate the main normalization evidence

4.3 Investigate reporter-expression dependence
This is an important refinement.
RAS uses mCherry as a continuous reference, but the ratio-of-sums estimator weights cells according to their reporter-associated red signal.

- Changes in reporter-positive population size.
- Changes in mCherry intensity distribution.
- Contributions from highly expressing cells.

N_{\mathrm{eff},R}
=
\frac{\left(\sum_i r_i\right)^2}

5. Phase III — RES as the Primary Scientific and Mathematical Contribution
Objective
This is the central task of the project.
Finalize Layer 2 as a complete empirical reporter-calibration module, with a defined primary model, executable forward and inverse functions, uncertainty estimation, calibration diagnostics, and real sample-level RES calculations.
The goal is to make RES a usable and reproducible reference-equivalent reporter measurement, while transparently describing its actual experimental support.
Do not stop at another calibration-feasibility report.
Do not merely compare three curves and conclude that external validation is insufficient.
Implement a complete operational model and evaluate it rigorously.
At the same time, do not manufacture validation or force biological conclusions unsupported by the data.
5.1 Preserve the correct scientific meaning of RES
The intended mathematical relationship is:
\[
RAS=g(A)
\]
where \(A\) is the reference-input composition.
RES is defined through the inverse calibration:
\[
\boxed{RES=g^{-1}(RAS)}
\]
The current S7 reference mixtures were prepared on an AC/INAC DNA mass-fraction basis.
Unless reliable construct lengths permit a verified molar conversion, use:
\[
A=A_{\mathrm{DNA,mass}}
\]
Accordingly, the output represents an AC reference-input equivalent under the fitted experimental calibration.
Do not present RES as an independently measured RNA-editing percentage.
Preserve the established name:
RES — Relative Editing Score
But explain its operational meaning precisely.
A suitable software label is:
RES — AC Reference-Equivalent Score
Include the actual input basis and model domain alongside every displayed numerical value.
5.2 Reproduce the corrected v4 calibration
Begin by reproducing the corrected S7 reference-response relationship.
The 75%/90% label correction must remain in effect.
Verify the original A/B well-level RAS measurements, corrected labels, and reference fractions.
Reproduce the existing model-comparison results.

The approximate reported values were:

5.3 Select and freeze a primary RES model
Treat the existing monotone hinge model as the leading candidate for the final operational RES model.

- Scientific interpretability.
- Model complexity.

A model should not be selected because it produces desired PUF rankings or convenient S10 editing predictions.
Mathematical definition
For a monotone hinge model, an appropriate general form is:
\[
g(A)
=
\alpha+\beta A+\gamma\max(0,A-k)
\]
where \(k\) is the hinge location.
The slopes on both sides must satisfy the declared monotonicity constraints.
Verify the actual model implemented in v4, including the knot-selection policy.
Do not silently retune the hinge location using the same evaluation data simply to obtain a lower reported error.

5.4 Establish the complete inverse-calibration procedure
Implement the inverse mapping explicitly.
For each input RAS, determine:
1. Whether the RAS falls within the calibration response range.
2. Whether the mapping is unique.
3. Whether the local response is sufficiently informative.
4. Whether the numerical inversion is stable.

Do not invent a unique point estimate for a flat calibration region.
Where the response lies outside the calibration range, do not silently extrapolate or clip to the nearest endpoint.
5.5 Quantify inverse-calibration performance
This analysis is more important than the forward \(R^2\).
Evaluate how accurately the model can recover the known reference-input fractions.
Use appropriate grouped internal validation.
Distinguish:
Leave-one-well-out
Evaluates performance on a withheld well when the same reference fraction may still be represented in training.
Leave-one-reference-level-out
Evaluates interpolation or prediction for a reference composition excluded from fitting.
Both are useful, but they answer different questions.
For inverse recovery, report:
- Mean absolute error in AC-fraction percentage points.
- RMSE in AC-fraction percentage points.
- Signed bias.
- Error distribution across the reference range.
- Fraction of held-out cases yielding a unique inverse.
- Fraction returning non-identifiable or out-of-range status.
- Dependence of error on AC input fraction.
- Uncertainty intervals and empirical coverage, where estimable.
The previous v4 analysis reported an inverse MAE of approximately 5.85 percentage points for a subset of held-out cases.
Verify the exact model, denominator, and selection conditions associated with that result.
Do not report inverse accuracy only for successfully inverted samples without also reporting the fraction of cases that failed to produce a valid inverse.
Otherwise, excluding difficult points could make calibration performance appear artificially strong.
Where possible, provide separate results for the full held-out set and the subset meeting explicitly stated inverse-identifiability requirements.
5.6 Propagate uncertainty into RES
Develop an appropriate uncertainty estimation procedure.
Consider:
- Variation between independent reference wells.
- RAS measurement uncertainty.
- Model parameter uncertainty.

\[
SE(RES)\approx
\frac{SE(RAS)}{|g'(RES)|}
\]
However, the final uncertainty procedure should also account for calibration-model uncertainty where supported.
Particularly investigate regions where the fitted response approaches a plateau.
